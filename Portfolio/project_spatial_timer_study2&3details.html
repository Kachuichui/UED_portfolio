Spatial Timer Prototype

Following the interviews in the previous Study (Study 1), I learned about people's thoughts on the kitchen AR technology. They struggled with "time" in various culinary tasks. A unique "spatial timer," Inspired by kitchen time management issues, it was created. This page will describe the "spatial timer," its prototype, and the experimental design to evaluate its effectiveness using multiple methods.

Design Inspiration

Traditional timers are often limited to a single device and a fixed location. This makes managing multiple tasks in a dynamic environment, like a kitchen, difficult and inefficient.

To overcome these limitations, I designed a Spatial Timer using Augmented Reality (AR). This transforms the concept of a timer from a 2D display into a 3D virtual object you can place anywhere in your physical space. Key features include:

  • Spatial Mapping: Linking time information directly to specific tasks or locations in the real world.
  • Spatial Interaction: Using natural hand gestures to control timers without touching a device.
  • Multi-Timer Collaboration: Effortlessly creating and managing multiple timers at once to handle complex, parallel tasks.

By bringing time management into our physical space, the Spatial Timer aims to make the process more intuitive, contextual, and efficient.

Spatial Timer – Key Functions

Spatial Timer Panel Function — time management is the primary purpose. I focused on the timer basics and included:

  1. • Time Input: Minute-level input via a digital keypad for fast entry.
  2. • Clear Buttons: Correct errors or adjust length quickly.
  3. • Start/Restart: Start countdown and restart from scratch anytime.
  4. • Pause/Resume: Pause and continue without losing progress.
  5. • Toggle: Switch between circular and slider countdown panels.
  6. • Delete: Remove finished timers to keep the space tidy.

Interactive Function Spatial Timer's gesture-based time management is innovative. In a three-dimensional environment, motions make timer generation, manipulation, and management easier. I created these primary gesture interactions:

  1. • Generate Timer: Our gesture was inspired by the British Sign Language "timer" Bsl (2013). Four fingers are bent to the same degree, but the thumb is bent somewhat differently. The user can generate a timer in the surrounding environment by performing this action.
  2. • Shift: Grab and drag movements move the timer anywhere in the space.
  3. • Zooming: Users may instantly reduce or enlarge the timer to fit various spaces using pinch and open actions.
  4. • Rotation: To improve timer spatial orientation, the user may squeeze one of the anchor points to rotate the timer at any angle around its vertical (Y-axis) and horizontal (X-axis) axes.
  5. • Time panel: Users may touch or poke the timer panel buttons to start, stop, reset, and swap timer forms.

Study 2

Core Objective: Evaluate the experience and usability of users creating and interacting with Spatial Timers.

Scenario Design & Implementation:

  • Built an AR environment using OVR Camera Rig (with Passthrough function for Meta Quest) to integrate Spatial Timers with the real world.
  • Deployed Spatial Timer prefabs and added debugging UI (hand model line renderers: green for valid gestures, red otherwise) to assist timer generation.

Observation Data: System Usability Scale (SUS)

  • Used a standardized SUS questionnaire.
  • Spatial Timer and Baseline Timer (traditional) shared the same structure for comparison.

Findings:

  • Spatial Timer showed a slight efficiency gain in timer generation tasks.
  • Challenges included spatial misalignment and inconsistent haptic/visual information.

Outcome

SUS Results: Spatial Timers outperformed single-device timers.

Study 3

Core Objective: Evaluate the effectiveness of the Spatial Timer in multi-task switching and attention management, and compare it with the traditional mobile phone timer.

Scenario Design & Implementation:

  • Set up 5 side-by-side Spatial Timer prefabs in 3D space for multi-task scenarios, adjusting positions based on laboratory size.
  • Developed "randomSelect.cs" script to randomly prompt 1 timer every minute (via sound/scale) for attention management.

Observation Data: NASA TLX Score, Post-experiment Interview, Affinity Diagram with Inductive Coding

  • NASA TLX: Evaluated via Pairwise Comparisons (weight) and Rating Scales, calculating Weighted Rating as Sum(Weight) / Sum(Weight*Rating).
  • Affinity Diagram: Categorized user feedback into themes like "Baseline Drawbacks" (distraction, bad time management) and "Spatial timers benefits" (visibility, intuitive, easy to operate).

Findings:

  • Impact on Workload: No statistically significant reductions in workload, but mean scores were slightly lower than single-device timers across 6 factors (Mental Demand, Physical Demand, etc.).
  • Interview Data: Highlighted intuitiveness of time perception, smoother attention switching in multitasking, and appreciation for interactive features/innovations.

Future Work

  • Expand research with More Sample Size to enhance statistical power.
  • Include Diverse user demographics to assess generalizability across different user groups.
